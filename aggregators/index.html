The field: the meta-aggregator

Revelation First ≠ Revelation Early. The pre-70 date is the old argument (Robinson, 1976; Gentry, 1989); Revelation First is the priority thesis: the rest of the New Testament unfolds from the Apocalypse.

The resources around the thesis, from manuscript collections and dating tables to models that read and date ancient texts and the record of how AI answers describe the thesis, each formalized as an entity. Every record answers one question: what evidence does each resource hold on when and from what the New Testament writings were composed, and how do machines now read those texts? 11 resources, compiled 2026-10-30; 7 more named and not yet read.

When was it written: the date of a work's composition, which no copy states. Dating tables and scholarly arguments propose it; a record says whose date it carries and on what ground.

From what: which text a text was made from. Parallels show that two texts share wording; the direction of dependence is an argument. Revelation First argues it runs from the Apocalypse outward.

In what copies: the surviving manuscripts, how each copy is dated (handwriting, radiocarbon, a model), and where it can be seen. A copy's date bounds its work's composition from one side only.

Read by machines: models that restore, place, date or find parallels for ancient texts, and the AI answers that now describe the field to most readers. Each is calibrated on a corpus, and the record names it.

A date of composition, an argument of dependence, a dated copy and a machine's reading each show a different part of the same question. Tap a step for what each kind of record can and cannot establish.

Four intersections

  • New Testament scholarship: dating, authorship and literary dependence
  • Papyrology and discovery: manuscripts, editions, new finds and redatings
  • AI and ancient texts: machine reading, restoration and dating
  • AI and scripture: how AI systems represent, cite and summarize sacred texts

Worked questions

A research question, the kind of evidence that can answer it, and the resources that hold that evidence.

Which papyri bear on the date of Revelation, and how is each one dated?

A papyrus dates its copy, and the composition must come before it. Most early New Testament papyri are dated by handwriting alone, so the method of dating matters as much as the date.

  • Center for the Study of New Testament Manuscripts
    images of P47, the Chester Beatty papyrus of Revelation, and of P46 (Paul), with century dates as catalogued
  • The Oxyrhynchus Papyri
    the largest collection of papyri from the principal find-site of early Christian papyri
  • Bodmer Lab
    the Bodmer papyri catalogue, in production
  • Enoch: dating ancient manuscripts with radiocarbon and AI-based writing-style analysis
    a method that moves palaeographic dates earlier against radiocarbon anchors, on the Dead Sea Scrolls

No record in this batch gives a sourced date and dating method for P47 or the other papyri of Revelation; the Münster Liste, which would, could not be read (pending).

Which manuscripts can be seen directly online, and which only through an edition?

An image lets a reader check a reading; an edition reports one. The difference decides what a reader can verify without the editor.

  • Center for the Study of New Testament Manuscripts
    images of Greek New Testament manuscripts, searchable by type and century
  • The Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library
    spectral images of Dead Sea Scroll fragments and 1950s negatives
  • Bodmer Lab
    digitized Bodmer papyri
  • The Oxyrhynchus Papyri
    images and the published editions of the Oxyrhynchus papyri

The Virtual Manuscript Room and the Codex Sinaiticus Project, the two largest image sources for the New Testament, could not be read from the build environment (pending).

What can machine dating of manuscripts establish, and what can it not?

Machine dating is calibrated on a corpus. What it establishes holds for that corpus and that kind of evidence.

  • Enoch: dating ancient manuscripts with radiocarbon and AI-based writing-style analysis
    style-based dates for the Dead Sea Scrolls trained on 24 radiocarbon samples, often older than palaeographic estimates
  • Ithaca
    restoration, place and date for Greek inscriptions, within 30 years of ground truth
  • Aeneas
    parallels for Latin inscriptions, the kind of evidence dependence arguments use
  • Vesuvius Challenge
    machine reading of unopened Herculaneum scrolls

None of the four dates a New Testament papyrus or a composition; each dates or reads copies within its own corpus.

How do AI systems describe Revelation First?

A machine answer is now the first account many readers meet. The record of those answers is the evidence; the answers are not evidence that what they say is so.

  • Capture Registry: the Revelation First addresses
    ten recorded addresses, June to October 2026, across Google, Perplexity and Grok, read against the thesis
  • AI and Faith
    a network discussing AI and religion; no measurement of answers

Signed-in and personalized answers are not recorded; the registry is the archive's own.
