The Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library

Papyrology and discoveryNew Testament scholarshipa digital library of spectral images of…active
The Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library: a digital library of spectral images of Dead Sea Scroll fragments and 1950s negatives, with introductions to the scrolls. Operator: the Israel Antiquities Authority (IAA), with the Leon Levy and Arcadia Funds. Its unit: a scroll fragment, imaged spectrally, with scans of the 1950s negatives (PAM). Its texts: the Dead Sea Scrolls: biblical, apocryphal and sectarian texts, mostly in Hebrew, with Aramaic and Greek.

Methodmultispectral imaging of the fragments; digitized negatives
Coveragegeography: Qumran and other Judaean Desert sites; period: scroll dates from the third century BCE to the first century CE at Qumran; further sites earlier and later
What it findsthe library preserves thousands of fragments, among them the oldest known copies of biblical texts; it dates the Qumran scrolls from the third century BCE to the first century CE, before the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • what a given scroll fragment looks like in high-resolution spectral images
  • the date range the library gives for the scrolls

Cannot answer

  • the composition of the New Testament writings
  • the date of a particular fragment (fragment records not opened)

In the worked questions

  • Which manuscripts can be seen directly online, and which only through an edition?
