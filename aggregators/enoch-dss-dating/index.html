Enoch: dating ancient manuscripts with radiocarbon and AI-based writing-style analysis

AI and ancient textsPapyrology and discoverya machine-learning date-prediction…historical
Enoch: dating ancient manuscripts with radiocarbon and AI-based writing-style analysis: a machine-learning date-prediction model for the Dead Sea Scrolls, trained on radiocarbon-dated samples, and the study that introduces it. Operator: Mladen Popović and colleagues (University of Groningen and partners), funded by the European Research Council; published in PLOS One. Its unit: a scroll's predicted date from the angular and allographic features of its handwriting. Its texts: the Dead Sea Scrolls.

What this evidence can settle

Can answer

  • how far palaeographic dates of the scrolls move against radiocarbon anchors
  • that a style-based model can date undated manuscripts within about thirty years on this corpus

Cannot answer

  • the dates of Greek New Testament papyri (not in its corpus)
  • when any work copied on a scroll was composed

In the worked questions

  • Which papyri bear on the date of Revelation, and how is each one dated?
  • What can machine dating of manuscripts establish, and what can it not?

Where it meets Revelation First

rd Dating: New Testament papyri are dated by palaeography alone, and this study moves palaeographic dates earlier against radiocarbon anchors. It does not date New Testament papyri; it is the method the papyrological-inversion and dating workstreams would watch.
