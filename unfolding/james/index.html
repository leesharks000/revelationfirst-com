The James Derivation

The executable granular case. Eleven seed elements from four verses. Nine rules, six of them one operation. Apply M, and the structural skeleton of the Epistle of James emerges — verse-matched, anchored, residue included.

The claim in operational form: the letter to Smyrna (Rev 2:8–11) is the seed; the Epistle of James is its unfolding. Not verbatim generation — structural derivation: every element of the seed maps through a named rule to a pericope of the epistle, two of the mappings ride lexical anchors that occur nowhere else in the canon, and the direction of dependency is visible in the verb forms. What the rules cannot produce is listed at the end, openly, as the falsification surface. Heteronym position: James — the poor, the tested, the diaspora synagōgē.

The rules

TRANSFORM M · 9 OPERATIONS · 3 FAMILIES
R1 EPISTOLARIZE
frame		superscription → epistolary prescript
unfold the sender-formula into a letter opening: named position + diaspora address
R2 THEOLOGIZE
expand		trial-event → doctrine of testing
unfold the event of trial into a theology of peirasmos: its joy, its product, its true agency
R3 CITE-PROMISE
anchor		performative future → received aorist
the promise made in the seed is cited in the unfolding as already-given: dōsō → epēngeilato
R4 SOCIALIZE
expand		inversion → social theology
unfold the four-word poverty–riches inversion into a doctrine of the poor and a judgment of the rich
R5 POSSESS
anchor		contested term → calm possession
the word fought over in the seed is simply used in the unfolding, without argument
R6 NARRATIVIZE
expand		bounded endurance → exempla
suffering-with-a-term becomes stories of patience: the farmer, the prophets, Job
R7 ECONOMIZE
expand		death-formula → death-economy
the second death unfolds into a full account of how death is produced and escaped
R8 IMPERATIVIZE
expand		fidelity command → paraenesis
one imperative becomes an ethics: endurance made complete, wanting nothing
R9 ADJUDICATE
expand		false claim → test of claims
the seed's 'say they are, and are not' becomes a procedure for testing what anyone says they are or have: faith said and not had, religion seemed and not kept
expand · R2, R4, R6, R7, R8, R9 — a compressed proposition unfolded into a process, its endpoint kept: condition → test → response → outcome
anchor · R3, R5 — a word or form carried across and changed in grammar or standing; these carry the direction of dependency
frame · R1 — the letter's sender-formula made an epistolary opening

The emissions

R1 EPISTOLARIZE← S1 + S4
Prescript: a named position writing to the scattered assembly
Jas 1:1 tais dōdeka phylais tais en tē diaspora
“James… to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad, greeting”
the diaspora address — the assembly Smyrna's letter fights for, written to as a whole
R2 THEOLOGIZE← S5 + S2
Doctrine of testing: trial has a product and a true agency
Jas 1:2–4 to dokimion hymōn tēs pisteōs katergazetai hypomonēn
“count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience”
peirasmos theologized: the seed's hina peirasthēte given its purpose-clause in full
Jas 1:13–14 mēdeis peirazomenos legetō hoti apo theou peirazomai
“Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God”
agency refined: the seed's devil-agency answered with a doctrine of where testing comes from
R3 CITE-PROMISE← S8 + S7
The promise, cited as received
Jas 1:12 lēmpsetai ton stephanon tēs zōēs, hon epēngeilato tois agapōsin auton
“he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him”
THE ARROW IN THE MORPHOLOGY: Rev 2:10 performs the promise (dōsō, first-person future); Jas 1:12 cites it (epēngeilato, aorist — promised, past). The only two occurrences of ton stephanon tēs zōēs in the canon. The epistle receipts the letter.
Jas 2:5 klēronomous tēs basileias hēs epēngeilato tois agapōsin auton
“heirs of the kingdom which he hath promised to them that love him”
the receipt clause of 1:12 a second time, word for word, now on the kingdom — in the verse that also carries the poverty–riches inversion (S3) and the hearing imperative (S11)
R4 SOCIALIZE← S3 + S5 + S10
Social theology: the poor elected, the rich withering, the rich as persecutors
Jas 2:5 tous ptōchous tō kosmō plousious en pistei
“Hath not God chosen the poor of this world rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom”
the seed's inversion, verbatim in its terms: ptōchos become plousios
Jas 2:6–7 helkousin hymas eis kritēria … blasphēmousin to kalon onoma
“Do not rich men oppress you, and draw you before the judgment seats? Do not they blaspheme that worthy name by the which ye are called?”
the seed's custody and blasphemy given to the rich: ballein … eis phylakēn (the devil, into prison) → helkousin … eis kritēria (the rich, into courts); blasphēmian (2:9) → blasphēmousin, the only blasphēm- in James
Jas 1:9–11 ho plousios… maranthēsetai
“let the brother of low degree rejoice in that he is exalted: but the rich, in that he is made low”
the inversion as standing doctrine
Jas 5:1–3 hoi plousioi, klausate
“Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl for your miseries”
the judgment side of the inversion, fully unfolded
R5 POSSESS← S4
The assembly, possessing its word
Jas 2:2 eis synagōgēn hymōn
“if there come unto your assembly a man with a gold ring”
synagōgē for the believing assembly — alone among the epistles. The seed's contested word, held without polemic.
R6 NARRATIVIZE← S6 + S7
Exempla of bounded patience
Jas 5:7–8 makrothymēsate… heōs tēs parousias
“Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth… be ye also patient”
endurance with a term: the ten days become the season until the rain
Jas 5:10–11 tēn hypomonēn Iōb ēkousate
“Ye have heard of the patience of Job, and have seen the end of the Lord”
bounded suffering narrativized — the trial that ends
R7 ECONOMIZE← S9
The economy of death
Jas 1:15 hē hamartia apotelestheisa apokyei thanaton
“and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death”
how the death of the formula is produced
Jas 5:20 sōsei psychēn autou ek thanatou
“shall save a soul from death, and shall hide a multitude of sins”
and how it is escaped — the epistle closes where the seed's promise pointed
R8 IMPERATIVIZE← S7 + S11
Paraenesis: endurance made complete; hearing made doing
Jas 1:4 hina ēte teleioi kai holoklēroi
“But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing”
the fidelity imperative unfolded into the epistle's whole ethic
Jas 1:22 ginesthe de poiētai logou kai mē akroatai monon
“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only”
the fidelity imperative's own verb, ginou → ginesthe, joined to the hearing formula: 'become faithful' + 'let him hear' → 'become doers of the word, not hearers only'
Jas 1:19, 1:25 tachys eis to akousai … ouk akroatēs epilēsmonēs … alla poiētēs ergou
“let every man be swift to hear … he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed”
the hearing formula unfolded into its process: hear, look into the law, continue, do, be blessed
R9 ADJUDICATE← S10
The test of claims
Jas 2:14 ean pistin legē tis echein erga de mē echē
“though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? can faith save him?”
legontōn … einai … kai ouk eisin → legē tis echein … mē echē: the saying, and the not-having, in the same order
Jas 1:26 ei tis dokei thrēskos einai
“If any man among you seem to be religious … this man's religion is vain”
the claimed identity (einai) tested against conduct

The arrow in the morphology

REV 2:10 — THE PROMISE PERFORMED (FIRST-PERSON FUTURE)
δώσω σοι τὸν στέφανον τῆς ζωῆς
↓
JAS 1:12 — THE PROMISE CITED (AORIST)
τὸν στέφανον τῆς ζωῆς, ὃν ἐπηγγείλατο τοῖς ἀγαπῶσιν αὐτόν
↓
JAS 2:5 — THE RECEIPT CLAUSE AGAIN, ON THE KINGDOM
τῆς βασιλείας ἧς ἐπηγγείλατο τοῖς ἀγαπῶσιν αὐτόν
"I will give thee the crown of life" → "the crown of life which he promised." A promise made, then a promise cited. The only two occurrences of the phrase in the canon, and the grammar carries the direction: performance, then receipt. James uses the receipt clause twice, the second time in the verse that holds Smyrna's poverty and riches.
